# Accor ALL → Airline Outbound Transfers — Captured & Verified (v1)

**Captured:** 2026-08-10
**Verified:** 2026-08-10, against Accor's own published conditions
**Status:** ✅ Core ratios verified against primary source · ⚠️ two items unresolved

**Source medium:** four social-media infographic cards supplied by the user
("31 Airline Partners", "Hack 1 — Iberia Plus", "Hack #2 — Flying Blue Promo
Rewards", "Hack #3 — The 1.25x Qantas Boost"). Uncredited, undated, video frames.

**Companion doc:** [`hdfc-transfer-partner-infographics-v1.md`](./hdfc-transfer-partner-infographics-v1.md)
covers the *inbound* side (HDFC → programs). This doc covers *outbound*
(Accor ALL → airlines).

---

## Headline finding

The infographics' **strategic framing is sound; their numbers are not.** Of the
three "hacks", one is real and actually understates its own benefit, one is
built on a transfer rate that is 2× too generous, and one is correct on ratio
but of doubtful availability from India.

**Nothing in this document should be seeded without the region check in §5.**

## 2. Hack 1 — Accor → Iberia Plus → Qatar ✅ VALID (and understated)

**Claimed:** Accor → Iberia 1:1, move Avios free, land in Qatar Privilege Club
with zero loss; versus a direct Accor → Qatar transfer of "2,000 ALL = 1,500
Avios (4:3), you lose ~25% value instantly."

**Verified:**

- Accor → Iberia Plus at **1:1 confirmed** (minimum 3,000 RP, not 2,000 as the
  card shows).
- **Avios moves free and 1:1** between British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus,
  Finnair and Qatar. Usually instant, up to 24h.
- **Correction to the infographic's own premise:** direct Accor → Qatar is
  **2,000 RP = 1,000 Miles (2:1)**, not 4:3. The direct route loses **50%**, not
  25%.

So the Iberia routing is **2× better than transferring direct** — the
infographic undersells its own hack by a factor of two. This is the one genuinely
strong finding in the set.

**Mechanical caveats the infographic glosses:**

- Qatar and Finnair are **not directly connected to Iberia**. Movement to/from
  Qatar routes through **British Airways as the hub** — handled inside the
  "Combine My Avios" tool at avios.com, but it is a two-stage move, not the
  single "move internally" step shown.
- All accounts must be in the **same name with matching personal details**, and
  **2FA must be enabled** on every account involved.
- Practical reports of BA↔Iberia linking failures despite matching details are
  common. Treat as a real-world friction, not a guaranteed path.

**Redemption claim (unverified):** Qatar Qsuite Mumbai → London/New York at
45,000–75,000 Avios against a ₹2.5L–₹4L cash fare, i.e. ~₹5/point. Award pricing
and surcharges were not checked.

## 3. Hack 2 — Flying Blue Promo Rewards ❌ PREMISE BROKEN

**Claimed:** 2,000 ALL = 2,000 Flying Blue miles (1:1), then wait for Promo
Rewards at 25–50% off, yielding ₹4–₹5+/point.

**Verified:** Accor → Flying Blue is **2,000 RP = 1,000 Miles (2:1)**, and it
requires an active **ALL × Flying Blue subscription**.

Flying Blue's Promo Rewards (monthly rotating discounted routes) is a real,
long-running feature and the 25–50% discount band is plausible. But **every
per-point figure on that card is computed from a transfer rate that is twice the
real one.** The claimed ₹4/point collapses to roughly ₹2/point once the actual
2:1 rate is applied — i.e. no better than Accor's own base value, before the
subscription requirement is even considered.

## 4. Hack 3 — the 1.25× Qantas boost ⚠️ RATIO OK, AVAILABILITY UNRESOLVED

**Claimed:** 2,000 ALL = 2,500 Qantas points (1:1.25), a built-in 25% bonus
before redeeming, best used for long-haul premium cabins via Emirates Skywards
and Cathay Asia Miles.

**Verified — and this is where the sources contradict each other:**

- Accor's **global airline-conditions page** lists Qantas automatic conversion
  as **discontinued as of 8 December 2021**.
- Accor's **Qantas partner page** currently advertises **2,000 ALL Reward Points
  → 2,500 Qantas Points**, minimum 2,000, with no stated residency restriction.
- Third-party coverage describes the partnership as active in 2026 and framed
  around the **Asia-Pacific / Australian** market.

So the **1:1.25 ratio is real** — the infographic is right about the number. What
is unresolved is **whether an India-registered ALL account can execute it.** The
Qantas partner page's full T&Cs were not reachable.

The infographic's redemption advice — avoid Qantas points for short domestic
economy (high carrier surcharges relative to low fares), use only for long-haul
premium — is directionally sound points-economics and consistent with Qantas's
well-known surcharge structure. Not independently verified here.

## 5. Open items — must resolve before any seeding

1. **Qantas from India.** Can an ALL account registered to an Indian address
   convert to Qantas Frequent Flyer? Resolves whether Hack 3 exists for our
   users at all. Highest priority — it's binary.
2. **Flying Blue subscription.** What is "ALL × Flying Blue", what does it cost,
   and is it available in India? Gates the Flying Blue link entirely.
3. **The ~20 unchecked partners** in §1 — most relevant to us are Emirates
   Skywards, Cathay Asia Miles, Etihad Guest and IndiGo BluChip.
4. ~~**Accor's ₹2/point base value**~~ — ✅ **DECIDED 2026-08-10.** Value Accor
   points at **₹2.00/point for calculations**. But the infographic's framing is
   **wrong**: Accor redemption value **is dynamic**, not a guaranteed floor with
   no dynamic pricing. ₹2.00 is a working assumption, not a contractual floor,
   and must not be narrated to users as "guaranteed". There is no point-value
   field in the catalog schema today, so this currently has no seed home — it is
   recorded in [`data/accor-outbound-transfers.yaml`](./data/accor-outbound-transfers.yaml)
   under `meta.accor_point_value_inr`.
5. **Qatar Qsuite award pricing** ex-India, including carrier surcharges.

## 6. What this means for the engines

**Valuation.** Accor's fixed redemption value makes it a clean worst-case anchor.
But the ₹4–₹5/point figures in these infographics are marketing arithmetic
resting on two inflated ratios — do not encode them as expected values. The one
defensible uplift path is Accor → Iberia → Qatar.

**Optimization.** This is the second independent example of the same structural
pattern flagged in the companion doc: **the best route is a multi-hop path that a
single-hop `currency → partner` model cannot represent.** Here the modeled direct
link (Accor → Qatar, 2:1) is exactly half as good as the real best route
(Accor → Iberia → Qatar, 1:1).

Two independent instances of the same gap is a strong signal it needs a design
decision rather than per-case workarounds. Both instances route through **Avios
as a shared currency**, which argues for modeling Avios as one currency with
member programs rather than adding a general path-search.

**Status (user decision 2026-08-10): multi-hop is confirmed as a FUTURE
capability, not MVP work.** Single-hop stands for now. The data has therefore
been staged in machine-usable form rather than left in prose:

> **[`data/accor-outbound-transfers.yaml`](./data/accor-outbound-transfers.yaml)**

That file mirrors `transfer_links.yaml`'s schema (`ratio_from`/`ratio_to`,
`source`, `verified_on`, `needs_verification`) so rows lift across with minimal
editing when multi-hop lands. It also encodes the **Avios cluster topology** —
including the fact that British Airways is the hub and that Qatar/Finnair are
reachable only through it, which a naive fully-connected path model would get
wrong. It is deliberately outside `backend/seeds/catalog/` and nothing loads it.
